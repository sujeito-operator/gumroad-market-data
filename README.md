## What the numbers say

- **A third of everything listed has never sold a measurable unit.** 33% with zero ratings is the background rate you compete against — and it held steady as the sample grew from 468 to 1511 products, so it is not an artefact of a small sample.
- **Game and 3D assets top the demand table.** VRChat avatars are the only category where every listing sampled has ratings; Unity assets 97%, Blender addons 94%, Procreate brushes 89% with a median of 136 ratings and a top product at 3,300.
- **Document, template and craft-pattern categories look busy and move slowly.** Excel dashboards and crochet patterns both 39% rated with medians of one and two ratings; resume templates, knitting patterns and pitch-deck templates all 42%.
- **Price and demand are close to unrelated.** The highest-demand categories are among the cheapest. Raising price is not the lever most sellers reach for it as.
- **Subscriptions are rare:** 70 of 1511 products bill recurring.
- **Price anchors:** median $29.7, 75th percentile $70.0, 90th $185.64.

## Method, and what this cannot tell you

42 searches were run against Gumroad Discover and the top results of each captured with a
headless browser: asking price, currency, subscription flag, rating count, title.

**Rating count is a proxy for units sold, not a sales figure** — only some buyers rate, and that
share differs by category. Use this to rank categories against each other rather than to estimate
revenue. It is one snapshot rather than a trend, and reflects the visible top of each category
rather than its full population. Nothing here is estimated or modelled beyond those limits.
