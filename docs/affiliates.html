What is true about the data, so you can say it without checking

The finding the report is built on, and the reason it is worth an audience's time: every Gumroad product page carries a state blob whose sales_count is a real unit count wherever the seller opted into showing it. So the ratings-to-sales multiplier can be measured rather than assumed. Across the 316 of 1,359 listings (23.3%) that disclose one, paid sales per rating run x25.5 (IQR x11.7-x54.2, n=190). Every commercial Gumroad-analytics tool sells revenue estimates built on an assumed multiplier and none of them publishes where theirs came from.

Underneath it: 4,532 sellers over 8,311 products, the top 1% hold 53.1% of all ratings, and 76.5% of sellers appear under exactly one category.

And what is not true about it, which matters more to you than to me

You would be putting your name on this in front of your own readers, so these belong above the button rather than under it.

  • The sales sub-sample is not a uniform draw of Gumroad. It is 1,359 product pages across 15 of the 15 top-level branches, and 57.4% of it sits in 3d. Read every sales figure as describing that mix.
  • Disclosure is opt-in, so sellers who switch the counter on are plausibly not a random sample. The multipliers are a lower bound.
  • Each category was crawled three pages deep, so a category's listing count here is a crawl depth, not a category size. Market-wide figures count each product once.
  • One snapshot, August 2026. Not a trend.
