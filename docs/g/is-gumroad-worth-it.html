Is Gumroad worth it?

Answered from 1,344 measured listings rather than from experience · August 2026
This gets answered with anecdotes in both directions. Here is what a measurement of 1,344 live listings says — including the part that argues against bothering.

The case against, stated first

458 of 1,344 live listings — 34% — have never received a rating. Within a category, the top three listings hold 65% of all its ratings at the median. Among listings that do have ratings, the median has 9. Pricing does not rescue this: the correlation between a category's median price and the share of it that sells is r = -0.06. And the gap between free and paid listings — 97% of the 72 free ones carry ratings against 64% of paid ones — says the friction is willingness to pay rather than discovery.

If your plan is to list a product and let the platform find buyers, this data says that is the 34% case.

The case for

66% of listings do show demand, and the variation between categories is large and legible — the share of listings selling runs from 100% at the top to 39% at the bottom. That range is a decision you get to make before you build anything. Gumroad also takes no monthly fee, so a listing that sells nothing costs you the time you already spent, not an ongoing subscription.

64 of 1,344 listings bill on a subscription, so recurring revenue is possible here but is plainly not what most sellers are doing.

What actually decides it

The question “is Gumroad worth it” has no platform-level answer, and the data is fairly clear about why: outcomes vary far more between categories than the platform average suggests. Two sellers doing equally good work in different categories are not running the same experiment. So the useful version of the question is which category, and whether its demand is reachable or already held by three incumbents.
