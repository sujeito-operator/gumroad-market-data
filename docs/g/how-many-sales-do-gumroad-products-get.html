How many sales do Gumroad products actually get?

The same 226 listings read on 2026-08-08 and again on 2026-08-26 · 18 days apart
Every other page on this site is a photograph of one day. This one is the difference between two photographs of the same product pages, which is the only way to answer whether anything is still happening on a listing. 26.5% of them sold nothing in 18 days.

The same 226 listings, read twice

On 2026-08-08 this project recorded the lifetime unit counter on every product page in its crawl that published one. On 2026-08-26 — 18 days later — it read the same pages again. 229 of them were still live; 226 were still publishing a counter at both readings, and those are the only rows compared below. Nothing here is an estimate: it is one number minus another number, and both CSVs are in the repository if you would rather do the subtraction yourself.

Which categories this covers. The listings behind these 226 product pages come from 15 of Gumroad's 15 top-level categories (3D, Audio, Business & Money, Comics & Graphic Novels, Design, Drawing & Painting, Education, Fiction Books, Films, Fitness & Health, Gaming, Music & Sound Design, Other, Photography, Recorded Music), so they are not an even draw across the platform. They are also unevenly weighted: 57% of them sit under 3D alone, against the 7% an even draw would give it. Weight them accordingly.

So 73.5% of these listings sold something in 18 days, which is the cheerful way to say it. The useful way is the first row: 26.5% sold nothing at all, and another 21.2% sold four units or fewer. Roughly a quarter of the set — 54 listings — moved twenty units or more.

The median listing that moved at all moved 10 units. Against its own lifetime total that is a change of 1.9%, so the median mover is a product that is ticking over rather than taking off. The largest single move in the window was 2,797 units.

Three things in this data that argue against it

10 counters went down. A lifetime unit total should not fall. Refunds and sellers resetting a counter both produce this and they cannot be told apart from outside, so 4.4% of the set is doing something the measurement cannot explain. It is reported rather than filtered out.

3 sellers switched the counter off between the two readings. That is the one change no single snapshot of this marketplace can show you, and it is the reason this page exists.

Only listings that publish a counter are in here at all. Publishing one is voluntary, and a seller who turns it on is likelier to be happy with the number. Read every figure above as the optimistic end of the marketplace, not the middle of it.

What did not move

Prices barely did: of the 177 listings priced in US dollars at both readings, 2 changed price in 18 days. (49 listings quoted in another currency are excluded — the earlier reading carries no currency column, and comparing across two would manufacture movement out of a unit mismatch.) Ratings moved on 68 of the 226, which is well under half the 166 that moved units — a reminder that ratings are a lagging and lossy proxy for sales, which is the subject of its own page here.

What this means if you are deciding whether to list something

The honest read is that an established listing on this platform is more likely to be quiet than busy, and that the difference between the quiet quarter and the busy quarter is not price — the correlation between a category's price and the share of it selling is close to zero. It is which category you are in and whether that category's demand is already held by its incumbents. The demand ranking is here and the case against bothering is here. Both are free.
