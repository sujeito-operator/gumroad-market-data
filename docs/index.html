A second, larger sample — 8,325 products and 4,545 sellers

Everything below this section comes from 42 Discover searches. Since then the whole of Gumroad's own category tree has been walked as a separate sample — 261 categories, 8,325 distinct products from 4,545 distinct sellers — and it is published in full beside this one. It is not a replacement: it is a different sampling frame, and where the two disagree the disagreement is the finding. This one puts the median paid asking price at $36.99; walking the taxonomy puts it at $18.03, because popular search terms never surface the cheaper depths of the catalogue.

It also records who is selling, which this sample never did. 3,263 of the 4,545 sellers have exactly one product, and the top tenth of sellers hold 89.2% of every rating measured.

And a third view: who is selling

Attributing every listing to its storefront turns the price table into a market structure, and the structure is the more interesting object. The top 1% of Gumroad sellers hold 52.5% of all measured demand — and the median seller in that 1% has 2 products. 14 of those 45 sellers have exactly one. The rank correlation between how much a seller lists and how much demand they attract is only 0.284, so whatever produces a winner here, listing more of them is not it.

What the numbers say

  • A third of everything listed has never sold a measurable unit. 34% with zero ratings is the background rate you compete against.
  • Creative assets dominate volume. vrchat avatar is the only category sampled where every listing has ratings; Procreate brushes carry a median of 136 with a top product at 3,300.
  • Template and document categories look busy and move slowly. excel dashboard sits at 39% rated with a median of 1 rating(s).
  • Price and demand are close to unrelated. The highest-demand categories are among the cheapest.
  • Subscriptions are rare: 64 of 1,344 products bill recurring.
  • Price anchors (USD): median $36.99, 75th percentile $87.99, 90th $222.01.

Method, and what this cannot tell you

42 searches were run against Gumroad Discover and the top results of each captured with a headless browser: asking price, the currency it was shown in, subscription flag, rating count, title. Rating count is a proxy for units sold, not a sales figure — only some buyers rate, and that share differs by category, so use this to rank categories against each other rather than to estimate revenue. It is one snapshot rather than a trend, and it reflects the visible top of each category rather than its full population.

On currency. Gumroad localises the prices it displays, so a single search returns a mixture: 1,237 in GBP, 228 in USD and 44 in EUR, with 40 of the 42 categories containing more than one. Prices here are converted to USD at ECB reference rates for 2026-08-06 (£1 = $1.3467, €1 = $1.1542); the raw price and its currency are both kept in the data so the conversion can be checked or redone.
